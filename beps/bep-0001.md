# BEP 1: The BEP process

## Summary

Bray Enhancement Proposals, or BEPs, record proposed changes, feedback, decisions, and the reasons for those decisions.
They cover Bray's language, official libraries and tooling, and project processes.

Bray's creator, [Anton B](https://github.com/lejmer), is the Benevolent Dictator for Life, or BDFL.
The BDFL holds final authority over language design and project governance and may delegate decisions.

This BEP defines the proposal types, statuses, document requirements, and review procedure.
Its status remains Draft until the BDFL adopts the process.

## Motivation and scope

Changes to Bray can affect existing programs and compiler implementations.
A BEP records the problem, proposed behavior, alternatives, costs, feedback, and decision.
Future contributors can read that record without searching through implementation reviews.

The process gives anyone a way to propose a change.
It names the people who review and decide proposals.
The amount of detail and review depends on the change's impact.

### Changes that require a BEP

A BEP is required for the following changes:

- Changes to specified language syntax or semantics, including removals and deprecations.
- Substantial changes to public interfaces or behavior in official libraries and tooling.
  These include new conventions, interoperability formats, and changes that affect compatibility.
- Changes to established governance, policies, or procedures, including this process.

Ordinary pull requests cover fixes that restore specified behavior, internal refactoring, performance improvements that
preserve behavior, and editorial documentation changes.

A compiler fix that restores an existing rule is maintenance.
An ambiguous specification may need a BEP if the resolution chooses new language behavior.
A maintainer may request a BEP for a borderline change.
The maintainer records the reason, and the BDFL resolves disputes about scope or admission.

Informational BEPs are optional.
They may document earlier design decisions as retrospective accounts.
Those accounts identify their purpose and support historical claims with available evidence.

### Goals and non-goals

BEPs make substantial proposals available for review and preserve the reasons for decisions.
They record both accepted proposals and the reasons for rejection.

Routine maintenance keeps its ordinary pull request workflow.
The [language reference](../docs/language/index.md) defines current specified behavior.
BEPs record proposed changes and the history of approved designs.
Compatibility analysis follows Bray's applicable compatibility policy.

## Proposal

### Governance and roles

Anyone may submit a BEP or contribute feedback.
Authors do not need a maintainer sponsor.

The author develops the proposal, updates the document, and responds to feedback.
The author records alternatives and substantial objections, including dissent.

An editor checks the scope, structure, and readability of a draft.
Editors assign numbers and maintain the metadata and index.
They also manage procedural status changes.
Admission as a Draft records that the document meets the submission requirements.

The decision-maker is the BDFL unless the BDFL authorizes a delegate.
The BDFL considers technical review, community feedback, and Bray's design goals when deciding a proposal.
Maintainership alone gives no authority to accept or reject a BEP.
Maintainers may make routine engineering decisions within established policy.

The BDFL records each delegation in public and defines its scope.
Each BEP names its decision-maker.
The BDFL retains final authority and resolves disputes about delegation, admission, and procedure.

One person may hold several roles.
The BDFL's own proposals follow the same document and review rules.

### Proposal types

Each BEP has one of the following types:

| Type | Purpose | Effect of acceptance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Technical | Propose changes to specified behavior or official interfaces. | The decision approves the design and its requirements. |
| Process | Propose changes to project rules, procedures, or governance. | The decision approves the rules, which apply from the recorded effective date. |
| Informational | Record explanations, analysis, design philosophy, or historical context. | The decision approves publication as a reference. |

Binding requirements belong in Technical or Process BEPs.
An Informational BEP may explain those requirements and link to their source.

### Statuses

All BEP types use the following statuses:

| Status | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Draft | The author develops the proposal and accepts feedback. |
| In review | The proposal has enough detail for formal review and a decision. |
| Accepted | The decision-maker has approved the proposal for its type. |
| Deferred | The decision-maker has paused consideration and recorded the reason and conditions for resuming review. |
| Rejected | The decision-maker has declined the proposal and recorded the reasons. |
| Withdrawn | The author has stopped pursuing a pending proposal before a decision. |
| Superseded | A later accepted BEP has fully replaced an accepted proposal. |

A proposal normally starts as Draft, enters In review, and reaches Accepted.
Review may also lead to rejection, deferral, or a return to Draft.
The author may withdraw a pending proposal.

The BDFL or delegate makes each acceptance, rejection, or deferral decision.
Editors record those decisions and may manage Draft and In review transitions.
A proposal keeps its status when discussion stops.

A rejected or deferred BEP may return to Draft or In review when new circumstances justify reconsideration.
The document records the reason for reopening and preserves earlier decisions.
The BDFL resolves disputes about reconsideration.

Accepted proposals remain part of the project record.
Changes to their design follow the amendment or supersession rules.

### Discussion and repository layout

BEP documents, the [index](README.md), and the [template](template.md) are in the
[official Bray repository](https://github.com/lejmer/bray), under `beps/`.
The [submission guide](submit-a-bep.md) describes the steps for authors.

Each admitted BEP has one canonical
[GitHub Discussion in that repository](https://github.com/lejmer/bray/discussions).
The thread belongs in the **BEPs** category.
Repository maintainers provide the category before the process takes effect.

An early discussion may become the canonical thread.
The thread keeps its URL across revisions, amendments, and pull requests.
Its title includes the BEP number after assignment.

The canonical discussion records design reasoning, alternatives, objections, review announcements, and decisions.
Pull requests submit and revise the document and allow inline feedback.
The author summarizes substantial design points from pull requests, meetings, or other discussions in the canonical thread.
The BEP preserves the decision rationale and links to the decision.

A document merge publishes the document's changes.
Design acceptance requires a separate, explicit decision.

Editors assign the next unused positive integer when admitting a draft.
BEP 1 identifies this process.
Numbers are permanent and independent of GitHub issue, discussion, and pull request numbers.
Editors never reuse a number.

An unnumbered submission uses `beps/bep-0000.md` and `bep: null`.
After assignment, the file is `beps/bep-NNNN.md`, with at least four digits in the number.
A title change leaves the number and filename unchanged.

### Submission and review

Early discussion is optional.
Authors may ask for feedback before writing a full proposal.

An editor checks a submitted draft for scope, structure, and readability.
Before admission, the draft has a permanent number and a link to its canonical GitHub Discussion.
Admission gives the document Draft status.
The BDFL resolves disputes about admission.

The author develops the proposal in public.
The author updates the document, responds to feedback, and records alternatives and substantial objections.

The author may request In review status once the document describes the behavior, tradeoffs, impact, and validation plan.
An editor checks completeness, and the decision-maker reviews the design.
Core design questions must be resolved before proposed acceptance.

When ready to decide, the decision-maker posts a proposed outcome in the canonical discussion.
The outcome may be acceptance, rejection, or deferral.
The post states the reasons, exact reviewed revision, and deadline.
The deadline includes a time zone.

The post opens a final comment period of 14 calendar days.
Changes to the design restart the period and identify the new reviewed revision.
Editorial corrections keep the existing deadline.

After reviewing final feedback, the decision-maker posts the decision and identifies the reviewed revision.
An editor updates the BEP's status, decision metadata, rationale, and index entry.
The document also records any implementation, release, or effective-date requirements.

The final comment period is part of In review.
The deadline itself produces no decision.
A proposal stays In review until the decision-maker publishes a decision or changes the review state.
The decision-maker may extend review or return the proposal to Draft.

The decision-maker may accept a proposal despite unresolved dissent.
The decision record explains why the chosen tradeoff fits Bray and addresses substantial objections.

For an expedited decision, the BDFL records why review was shortened and what review opportunity was provided.
The decision still identifies the exact revision and preserves the reasons for the outcome.

### Document format and metadata

BEPs are Markdown files with YAML front matter.
The [template](template.md) contains the required sections and fields.

#### Writing rules

This is mostly aimed at agents, but applies to everyone.
BEP prose follows the [Unslop](https://github.com/cursor/plugins/blob/main/pstack/skills/unslop/SKILL.md)
and [Technical writing](https://github.com/cursor/plugins/blob/main/pstack/skills/technical-writing/SKILL.md) skills.
Prose contains no semicolons.

#### Common fields

Every BEP has the following fields:

| Field | Value |
| --- | --- |
| `bep` | The assigned positive integer, or `null` before assignment. |
| `title` | The proposal title. |
| `type` | `Technical`, `Process`, or `Informational`. |
| `status` | One of the seven statuses above, with the same spelling. |
| `authors` | A list of author names or GitHub handles. |
| `created` | The creation date as a quoted `YYYY-MM-DD` string. |
| `discussion` | The canonical GitHub Discussion URL, or `null` in a submission before admission. |
| `decision_maker` | The name of the BDFL or authorized delegate. |
| `requires` | A list of BEP numbers on which the proposal depends. |
| `supersedes` | A list of BEP numbers the proposal replaces. The body defines the extent of replacement. |
| `superseded_by` | A list of accepted successor BEP numbers. The body defines any partial replacement. |
| `decision` | A permalink to the most recent formal decision, or `null` before a decision. |
| `decided` | The date of that decision as a quoted `YYYY-MM-DD` string, or `null` before a decision. |
| `approved_revision` | The full Git commit SHA of the most recently accepted text, or `null` before acceptance. |

Absent relationships use empty lists.
The Decision and history section preserves earlier decisions.
Each decision post identifies the exact revision it resolves.
A later metadata update may link to that revision.

#### Fields for implementation and release details

Technical BEPs have the following fields:

| Field | Value |
| --- | --- |
| `implementation_status` | `Not started`, `In progress`, or `Complete`. |
| `implementation` | A list of links to implementation work. |
| `experimental_since` | The first experimental release, or `null` before experimental availability. |
| `first_stable_release` | The first stable release, or `null` before stable availability. |

Process BEPs have an `effective` field for the effective date.
Informational BEPs have a `published` field for the approved publication date.
Both fields use a quoted `YYYY-MM-DD` string, or `null` before the date is determined.

Implementation progress and release availability are separate from the proposal's status.
Acceptance authorizes work toward inclusion.
It carries no release deadline or implementation assignment.

An experimental release states its stability expectations.
Before a stable release, the BDFL or delegate records a readiness decision against the proposal's validation and release criteria.

### Required content

Every BEP has the following eight sections.
The amount of detail depends on the proposal's impact.
For a section or consideration that does not apply, the author explains why.

| Section | Required content |
| --- | --- |
| Summary | The section explains the proposal and its intended result. |
| Motivation and scope | The section describes the problem, affected audience, examples, goals, and non-goals. |
| Proposal | The section defines the proposed change or presents the informational content. |
| Rationale and alternatives | The section explains the approach, drawbacks, alternatives, and the option of keeping current behavior. |
| Impact | The section describes effects on users, implementations, tooling, and project practices, including compatibility and migration where relevant. |
| Validation and adoption | The section explains how to evaluate the proposal and put it into effect. |
| Open questions | The section lists unresolved questions and decisions. |
| Decision and history | The section records outcomes, reasons, substantial objections, reconsideration, and amendments. |

Technical BEPs define syntax, semantics, interfaces, errors, edge cases, and interactions with existing features where relevant.
They address compatibility, security, performance, and interoperability as applicable.
They include a test plan, a release plan, and proposed changes to the language reference where relevant.

The specification has enough detail for another implementer to implement the design without making new language decisions.
Examples illustrate the specification.
The specification also defines behavior beyond the examples.

Process BEPs define roles, procedures, exceptions, transition steps, and when rules take effect.

Informational BEPs identify their audience and explain the evidence, conclusions, and limitations.
Retrospective accounts identify themselves as such.

Drafts may contain placeholders and unresolved questions.
Acceptance requires complete sections for the decision and resolution of core design questions.
Follow-up implementation details may remain open if they cannot change the approved design.

### Maintenance, amendments, and supersession

BEPs keep their documents and numbers after rejection, withdrawal, or supersession.

Ordinary pull requests may correct wording, add implementation links, or update implementation and release metadata.
Those edits preserve the approved design's meaning and history.

Changes to an accepted design require renewed review and an explicit decision against the revised text.
A bounded amendment may use the same BEP if it preserves the proposal's purpose.
A replacement design or an independently substantial change uses a new BEP.
The BDFL resolves boundary questions.

The previously approved revision remains authoritative until the decision-maker accepts an amendment or successor.
The Decision and history section records amendments and their reasons.
Acceptance updates the approved revision.

A successor identifies what it replaces and links both documents.
Full replacement changes the earlier BEP's status to Superseded.
Partial replacement preserves the earlier BEP's applicable status and identifies the affected sections and successor.

The language reference and project policies incorporate approved changes as they take effect.
BEPs retain the design and decision history.

## Rationale and alternatives

The BDFL model names who makes each decision as the contributor group grows.
Editors maintain the documents while the BDFL or delegate decides the design.

A shared set of statuses gives each proposal type the same decision states.
Separate implementation and release fields show whether an accepted design is available to users.

One canonical discussion keeps feedback together across revisions.
Pull requests support review of the text, and the BEP records the final reasons.

A final comment period of 14 calendar days gives contributors time to review an intended decision.
The expedited path allows shorter review with a public explanation.
Documents and review take work and can delay a decision.
Limiting BEPs to substantial changes keeps that cost focused on decisions that need a lasting record.

Ordinary pull requests alone can spread a design's reasons across implementation reviews.
Requiring a BEP for every edit would add work to routine maintenance.
A voting process would need rules for participation and tie-breaking.
This process instead names the BDFL as the final authority.

## Impact

Contributors have a defined place to propose substantial changes and read decisions.
Authors maintain proposals and summarize feedback.
Editors manage admission, numbering, metadata, and the index.

Maintainers provide a BEPs category in the official repository's GitHub Discussions.
From the recorded effective date, substantial changes follow this process.
Routine maintenance keeps its existing pull request workflow.

This process defines project rules.
It changes no language semantics, public compiler interfaces, or runtime behavior.
Each Technical BEP addresses security and performance where relevant.

## Validation and adoption

Review of this process covers an actual proposal workflow.
The workflow includes draft admission, canonical discussion, formal review, final comment, a decision, and metadata and index updates.

Before adoption, maintainers provide the BEPs discussion category.
Adoption requires a canonical discussion for this proposal and a populated `discussion` field.
The BDFL records a decision against the reviewed Git revision in that discussion.

On acceptance, the header and history record the decision permalink, date, approved revision, and effective date.
An editor updates the index.
The effective date determines when substantial changes must follow the process.

Earlier language designs need no retroactive proposals.
Authors may submit useful historical explanations as Informational BEPs that identify themselves as retrospective accounts.

Changes to this process follow the same review and decision rules.
The BDFL's own proposals follow those rules.
Expedited adoption includes the required public explanation and review opportunity.
