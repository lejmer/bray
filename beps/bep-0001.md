# BEP 1: The BEP Process

## Summary

Bray Enhancement Proposals (BEPs) provide a public, versioned way to propose substantial changes, obtain feedback, and
preserve design decisions. They cover Bray's language, official libraries and tooling, and project processes.

Bray is led by its creator, Anton B ([@lejmer](https://github.com/lejmer)), as Benevolent Dictator for Life (BDFL).
The BDFL holds final authority over language design and project governance and may explicitly delegate decisions.

This BEP defines the proposal types, statuses, document requirements, and review procedure.
It is a Draft until formally adopted under the adoption rules below.

## Motivation and scope

Language changes create lasting consequences for users and implementers. A BEP records the problem, proposed behavior,
alternatives, costs, feedback, and reasons for the decision in a place future contributors can find.

The process supports open participation, clear decision authority, and review proportional to a change's impact.
It aims to make substantial decisions understandable and durable while keeping routine maintenance straightforward.

### When a BEP is required

- Changes to specified language syntax or semantics, including removals and deprecations.
- Substantial changes to public interfaces or behavior in official libraries and tooling, including new conventions,
  interoperability formats, and compatibility consequences.
- Changes to established project governance, policies, or procedures, including the BEP process itself.

Fixes that restore specified behavior, internal refactoring, performance improvements that preserve behavior, and
editorial documentation changes use ordinary pull requests.

Correcting a compiler to follow an existing rule is maintenance. Resolving an ambiguous specification may require a BEP
when it chooses substantive language behavior. A maintainer may request a BEP for a borderline change and must explain
why. The BDFL resolves disagreements about scope or admission.

Informational BEPs are optional. Existing design decisions may be documented retrospectively, with that purpose clearly
identified and historical claims supported by available evidence.

### Goals and non-goals

BEPs should make substantial proposals reviewable, record the reasons for decisions, and define who can decide.
They preserve both successful proposals and the reasoning behind decisions against proposals.

Routine engineering work remains in the ordinary pull request workflow. The language reference documents current
specified behavior; BEPs preserve proposed changes and the history and rationale of approved designs.
Compatibility analysis follows Bray's applicable compatibility policy.

## Proposal

### Governance and responsibilities

Anyone may submit a BEP or contribute feedback. A maintainer sponsor is not required.

- **Author or champion:** develop the proposal, incorporate feedback, maintain the document, and fairly summarize
  alternatives and substantial objections.
- **Editor:** check scope, structure, and readability; assign numbers; maintain metadata and the index; and manage
  procedural status updates. Editorial admission is not a decision on the design.
- **Decision-maker:** the BDFL by default, or a person explicitly authorized by the BDFL for the proposal or a defined area.

The BDFL considers technical review, community feedback, and Bray's design goals when making decisions.
Maintainership alone confers no authority to accept or reject a BEP. Maintainers may make routine engineering decisions
within established project policy.

Delegation must be public and identify its scope. Each BEP names its decision-maker.
The BDFL retains final authority and resolves disputes about delegation, admission, and procedure.
One person may occupy several roles. The BDFL's own proposals follow the same document and review requirements.

### Proposal types

| Type | Purpose | Effect of acceptance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Technical | Change specified behavior or official interfaces, such as syntax, semantics, libraries, tooling, or package formats. | Approve the documented design and applicable requirements. |
| Process | Establish or change project procedures, governance, release policy, or other project rules. | Approve the process requirements, effective on the recorded date. |
| Informational | Preserve explanations, analysis, design philosophy, or historical context. | Approve publication as a reference. |

Binding requirements belong in Technical or Process BEPs. An Informational BEP may explain such requirements and link to
their authoritative source.

### Statuses

| Status | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Draft | The proposal is being developed and is open to feedback. |
| In review | The proposal is sufficiently complete for formal consideration and a decision. |
| Accepted | The designated decision-maker has approved the proposal for its type. |
| Deferred | Consideration is explicitly paused, with a reason and conditions for resuming. |
| Rejected | The decision-maker has declined the proposal, with recorded rationale. |
| Withdrawn | The author has stopped pursuing a pending proposal before a decision. |
| Superseded | A later accepted BEP has fully replaced an accepted proposal. |

The normal progression is Draft to In review to Accepted. Review may instead lead to rejection, deferral, or further
drafting. Authors can withdraw pending proposals. Accepted proposals become part of the project decision record and
change through amendment or supersession.

Acceptance, rejection, and deferral require an explicit decision from the BDFL or the authorized delegate.
Editors record those decisions and may manage Draft and In review transitions as procedural updates.
Inactivity never implies rejection or acceptance.

A rejected or deferred proposal can return to Draft or In review when circumstances warrant reconsideration.
Record the reason for reopening and preserve its prior decisions. The BDFL resolves disputes about reconsideration.

### Discussion and repository layout

BEP documents, their [index](README.md), and the [template](template.md) live in the official
[`lejmer/bray` repository](https://github.com/lejmer/bray), under `beps/`.

Each admitted proposal has one canonical
[GitHub Discussion in that repository](https://github.com/lejmer/bray/discussions), in the **BEPs** category.
Repository maintainers provide that category as part of adopting the process.
An early discussion may become the canonical thread. Add the BEP number to its title when assigned.

The canonical discussion holds design reasoning, alternatives, objections, review announcements, and decisions.
Pull requests submit and revise the document and support inline feedback. Summarize substantial design points from
pull requests, meetings, or elsewhere in the canonical discussion.

A proposal retains its discussion across revisions, amendments, and multiple pull requests.
The BEP preserves the decision rationale and links to the formal decision.
Merging a document or changing a pull request's state does not constitute design acceptance.

Editors assign the next unused positive integer when admitting a draft. BEP 1 identifies this process.
Numbers are permanent, are never reused, and are independent of GitHub issue, discussion, and pull request numbers.
Use `bep-0000.md` and `bep: null` for an unnumbered submission, then rename it to `bep-NNNN.md` with at least four digits
when a number is assigned. Titles may change without changing the number or filename.

### Submission and review

1. **Optional early discussion.** Authors may discuss the problem before writing a full BEP. Early feedback is encouraged
   for substantial changes and remains optional.
2. **Submit a draft.** An editor checks scope, basic structure, and readability, assigns a permanent number, and admits
   the document as Draft. The submission must link its canonical GitHub Discussion before admission.
   The BDFL resolves admission disputes.
3. **Develop the proposal publicly.** The author updates the document, addresses feedback, and summarizes alternatives
   and significant objections in the canonical discussion.
4. **Request formal review.** The author requests In review status when behavior, tradeoffs, impact, and validation are
   sufficiently described for a decision. An editor checks completeness; the decision-maker assesses the design.
   Core design questions must be resolved before proposed acceptance.
5. **Announce a proposed outcome.** The decision-maker posts proposed acceptance, rejection, or deferral, with rationale,
   an exact reviewed revision, and a deadline, in the canonical discussion. This opens a 14-calendar-day final comment
   period. State the deadline with an explicit time zone so the review interval is unambiguous.
6. **Publish the decision.** After considering final feedback, the decision-maker posts the outcome in the canonical
   discussion, identifying the reviewed revision. Update the BEP's status, decision metadata, rationale, and applicable
   implementation, release, or effective-date requirements, and update the index.

The final comment period is a step within In review. Material design changes restart the period and identify the new
reviewed revision; editorial corrections do not.

A deadline never produces automatic approval. The proposal remains In review until an explicit decision is published.
The decision-maker may extend review or return the proposal to Draft when further work is needed.

Acceptance can proceed with unresolved dissent. The decision record must explain why the chosen tradeoff is appropriate
for Bray and how substantial objections were addressed.

For an expedited decision, the BDFL must publicly record why review was shortened and what review opportunity was
provided. The other requirements for an explicit, revision-specific decision and a durable rationale still apply.

### Document format and metadata

BEPs are Markdown files with YAML front matter. Use the [template](template.md).
The header records the following fields:

| Field | Value |
| --- | --- |
| `bep` | Assigned positive integer; `null` before assignment. |
| `title` | Proposal title. |
| `type` | `Technical`, `Process`, or `Informational`. |
| `status` | One of the seven statuses above, with the same spelling. |
| `authors` | List of author names or GitHub handles. |
| `created` | Creation date as a quoted `YYYY-MM-DD` string. |
| `discussion` | Canonical GitHub Discussion URL; may be `null` in a submission before admission. |
| `decision_maker` | Named BDFL or authorized delegate. |
| `requires` | List of BEP numbers on which this proposal depends. |
| `supersedes` | List of BEP numbers this proposal replaces, with the extent explained in the body. |
| `superseded_by` | List of accepted successor BEP numbers, with any partial replacement explained in the body. |
| `decision` | Permalink to the most recent formal decision, or `null` while none exists. |
| `decided` | Date of that decision as a quoted `YYYY-MM-DD` string, or `null`. |
| `approved_revision` | Full Git commit SHA identifying the most recently accepted text, or `null` before acceptance. |

Use empty lists for absent relationships. Preserve earlier decisions in the document's Decision and history section.
Decision posts must identify the exact revision they resolve; a subsequent metadata update can link to that revision.

Add the applicable type-specific fields:

| Type | Fields |
| --- | --- |
| Technical | `implementation_status`: `Not started`, `In progress`, or `Complete`; `implementation`: implementation links; `experimental_since`: first experimental release or `null`; `first_stable_release`: first stable release or `null`. |
| Process | `effective`: effective date as a quoted `YYYY-MM-DD` string, or `null` before it is determined. |
| Informational | `published`: approved publication date as a quoted `YYYY-MM-DD` string, or `null` before publication. |

Implementation progress and release availability are separate from the proposal's status. Acceptance authorizes work
toward inclusion without promising a release date or assigning implementation work to anyone.
Experimental availability must state its stability expectations. Stable inclusion requires a recorded readiness decision
by the BDFL or authorized delegate against the proposal's validation and release criteria.

### Required content

Every BEP uses these eight sections. Scale their depth to the proposal's impact.
If a section or consideration does not apply, briefly explain why.

| Section | Required content |
| --- | --- |
| Summary | A short explanation of the proposal and intended result. |
| Motivation and scope | The problem, affected audience, concrete examples, goals, and non-goals. |
| Proposal | The complete proposed change or informational content. |
| Rationale and alternatives | Reasons for the approach, drawbacks, alternatives, and the option of retaining current behavior. |
| Impact | Effects on users, implementations, tooling, and project practices, including compatibility and migration where relevant. |
| Validation and adoption | How the proposal will be evaluated and put into effect. |
| Open questions | Remaining uncertainties and decisions. |
| Decision and history | Outcomes, rationale, substantial objections, reconsideration, and amendments. |

Technical BEPs must specify syntax, semantics, interfaces, errors, edge cases, and interactions with existing features
where relevant. Address compatibility, security, performance, and interoperability as applicable, and provide a testing
and rollout plan. Include proposed edits to the [language reference](../docs/language/index.md) where relevant.
The specification must be precise enough for another implementer to implement the design without inventing major
behavior. Examples illustrate the specification, which must also define behavior beyond those examples.

Process BEPs must define responsibilities, procedures, exceptions, transition arrangements, and when rules take effect.

Informational BEPs must identify their intended audience and explain the supporting evidence, conclusions, and
limitations. Retrospective accounts must be identified as such.

Drafts may contain placeholders and unresolved questions. Acceptance requires complete decision-relevant sections and
resolution of core design questions. Follow-up implementation details may remain open when they cannot materially alter
the approved design.

### Maintenance, amendments, and supersession

Preserve documents and their numbers after rejection, withdrawal, or supersession.

Editorial corrections, implementation links, and accurate delivery metadata can be updated through ordinary pull
requests. They must preserve the meaning and history of the approved design.

Material changes to an accepted design require renewed review and an explicit decision against the revised text.
Submit a bounded amendment to the same BEP when it preserves the proposal's purpose; use a new BEP for a replacement
design or an independently substantial change. The BDFL resolves boundary questions.
The previously approved revision remains authoritative for that decision until an amendment or successor is accepted.

Record amendments and their rationale in Decision and history and update the approved revision after acceptance.
A successor identifies what it replaces. Mark the earlier BEP Superseded when fully replaced, linking both documents.
For partial replacement, retain the earlier BEP's applicable status and identify the affected sections and successor.

The current language reference and project policies must incorporate approved changes as they take effect.
BEPs retain their role as design and decision records.

## Rationale and alternatives

Open participation and a documented BDFL decision preserve clear accountability as the contributor group grows.
Editors can keep documents organized while design authority remains explicit.

A common lifecycle gives readers consistent status meanings across proposal types. Separate delivery metadata prevents
a design decision from being mistaken for implementation or stable availability.

One canonical discussion keeps feedback discoverable across document revisions. Pull requests provide focused text
review, while the BEP preserves the lasting rationale.

A 14-calendar-day final comment period gives contributors a predictable opportunity to review an intended decision.
The expedited path permits shorter review with a public explanation. Formal documents and review introduce work and can
delay decisions; the scope threshold and proportional document depth keep that cost focused on substantial changes.

Ordinary pull requests alone are suitable for maintenance but often spread the reasoning for substantial decisions across
implementation details. Requiring a BEP for every edit would add unnecessary work. A voting-based process would require a
different governance model and rules for participation and tie-breaking. This process makes BDFL authority explicit.

## Impact

Contributors gain a consistent place to propose substantial changes and understand decisions.
Authors maintain proposals and summarize feedback; editors manage admission, numbering, metadata, and the index.

Maintainers need a BEPs category in the official repository's enabled GitHub Discussions.
Substantial decisions use the procedure from the recorded effective date. Routine maintenance keeps its existing pull
request workflow.

This process establishes no new language semantics, public compiler interfaces, or runtime behavior.
Security and performance considerations are assessed by individual Technical BEPs where relevant.

## Validation and adoption

Review this process using its document requirements and an actual proposal workflow: draft admission, canonical
discussion, formal review, final comment, decision recording, and a corresponding metadata and index update.

Before adoption, provide the BEPs discussion category, create this proposal's canonical discussion, and fill its
`discussion` field. The BDFL records an explicit decision against the reviewed Git revision in that discussion.
On acceptance, record the decision permalink, date, approved revision, and effective date in the header and history,
and update the index.

The effective date determines when substantial changes must follow the process.
Existing language design does not require retroactive proposals. Useful historical explanations may be submitted as
clearly identified Informational BEPs.

Amendments to this process follow the same review and decision rules. The BDFL's own proposals use those rules as well.
Any expedited adoption must include the required public explanation and review opportunity.
