## Summary

Briefly explain the proposal and its intended result.

## Motivation and scope

Describe the problem and affected audience. Give concrete examples, goals, and non-goals.
For a retrospective Informational BEP, identify it as a retrospective account.

## Proposal

For a Technical BEP, explain the change for users and then specify its syntax, semantics, interfaces, error behavior,
edge cases, and interactions with existing features. Include proposed language-reference changes where relevant.
Make the behavior precise enough for another implementer to implement it without inventing major semantics.

For a Process BEP, define responsibilities, procedures, exceptions, transition arrangements, and when the rules take effect.

For an Informational BEP, present the explanation or analysis, its evidence, conclusions, and limitations.
Binding technical or process requirements belong in a Technical or Process BEP.

## Rationale and alternatives

Explain why this approach is appropriate for Bray. Describe drawbacks and alternatives, including retaining the current
behavior. Summarize substantial objections fairly, including dissent that remains after a decision.

## Impact

Describe effects on users, implementations, official libraries and tooling, and project practices.
Address compatibility, migration, security, performance, and interoperability where relevant.
Follow Bray's applicable compatibility policy. Briefly explain why a consideration does not apply.

## Validation and adoption

Explain how the proposal will be evaluated and put into effect.

For a Technical BEP, describe relevant tests, prototype evidence, implementation work, experimental availability,
documentation updates, and stable-release readiness criteria.

For a Process BEP, explain the transition, responsible roles, and effective date.

For an Informational BEP, identify the intended audience and how evidence and conclusions will be reviewed for publication.

## Open questions

List unresolved questions and distinguish core design decisions from follow-up implementation details.
Before acceptance, resolve questions that could materially change the approved design.
If none remain, say so.

## Decision and history

While undecided, state that a decision is pending.

Record each decision or reconsideration with its date, decision-maker, outcome, canonical discussion permalink, and exact
reviewed revision. Preserve the rationale and responses to significant objections in the document.
Record follow-up requirements, amendments, and any supersession. Keep earlier decisions visible.
